The Birkman Method

When you are at a career crossroads, the hardest question is often not how to make a move.

It is knowing which direction makes sense in the first place.

The Birkman Method is an in-depth assessment that helps us look beyond job titles and past experience to understand the kind of work, environment and conditions in which you are most likely to be engaged, effective and satisfied.

What Birkman helps us understand

Birkman provides insight into several different aspects of how you work and what motivates you, including:

  • the activities and areas that naturally interest you

  • how you typically behave and work with others

  • the conditions and expectations you need in order to perform at your best

  • how your behaviour may change when those needs are not being met

  • the types of work and career environments that may be worth exploring.

This gives us a much richer starting point than simply asking, “What job should I do next?”

Why I use it for career direction

Career decisions become difficult when experience creates lots of possible options but little clarity about which ones are genuinely right.

Birkman gives us a structured way to separate:

what you can do
from
what you are likely to find motivating and sustainable.

That distinction can be particularly valuable for experienced professionals who have spent years succeeding in roles that they may no longer want to repeat.

The assessment does not make the career decision for you.

Instead, it gives us better information to work with.

What the process involves

You complete the Birkman questionnaire online at your own pace. Most people allow around an hour, although it is not timed.

I then generate and review your personalised Birkman reports before we meet.

We usually work through the findings over two career direction consultations of around 60–90 minutes each.

The objective is not simply to explain a report.

It is to use the insights to help answer practical career questions such as:

  • What kind of work is most likely to engage me?

  • Where are my strengths most useful?

  • What working environments are likely to suit me?

  • What might be contributing to frustration or dissatisfaction in my current situation?

  • Which career directions are worth investigating further?

  • What should I be cautious about repeating in my next move?

More than a personality profile

One of the reasons I have continued to use Birkman for more than two decades is the depth of information it gives us.

It looks not only at observable behaviour, but also at underlying needs and interests — including factors that may not be obvious to colleagues or even to you.

That makes it particularly useful when the challenge is not simply finding another role, but deciding what the next stage of your career should look like.

Experience matters in the interpretation

An assessment is most useful when it is interpreted in context.

I have worked with the Birkman Method for more than 20 years and use it as part of a broader career advisory conversation.

We look at the results alongside your experience, current circumstances, ambitions, constraints and the realities of the market.

The goal is not to put you into a category.

It is to create greater clarity about the work and environments in which your experience is most likely to translate into a satisfying next chapter.
